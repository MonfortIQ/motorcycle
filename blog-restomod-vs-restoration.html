Restomod vs Full Motorcycle Restoration

A restoration preserves originality while a restomod adds selected modern improvements. The right path depends on history, use and ownership goals.

Apex & Iron Workshop July 19, 2026 7 min read

Restoration and restomod projects can begin with the same motorcycle but finish with different priorities. Decide what should remain original before parts are sourced or removed.

In this article

  1. 01Define the ownership goal
  2. 02Document what is original
  3. 03Choose upgrades with a reason
  4. 04Compare scope and parts sourcing
  5. 05Keep the result serviceable

Define the ownership goal

A period-correct restoration may prioritize factory finishes, correct hardware and provenance. A restomod may keep the character of the motorcycle while improving starting, lighting, brakes or suspension.

Neither approach is automatically more valuable or reliable. The condition, rarity and documentation of the donor influence the decision.

Document what is original

Before teardown, we record frame numbers, castings, paint evidence, markings and unusual components. Those details can affect a restoration’s historical value.

When originality matters, repairable factory parts may be more important than convenient replacements. Owners should approve irreversible changes in advance.

Choose upgrades with a reason

A restomod can benefit from modern tires, improved lighting, charging capacity or braking components when they suit the motorcycle and are integrated carefully.

Visible upgrades should not compromise fit, service access or the machine’s intended use. We make a list of gains and trade-offs before selecting parts.

Compare scope and parts sourcing

Restoration may need scarce original components and specialist finish work. A restomod may require engineering, fabrication and validation of newer parts.

Both paths benefit from a documented budget with contingency for hidden wear discovered during disassembly.

Keep the result serviceable

A clear parts record, wiring diagram and before-and-after photographs help preserve the project history. For a restomod, record every non-original system and service requirement.

A successful handover explains what changed, why it changed and how to maintain the motorcycle.

Workshop checklist

  • Record numbers and original finishes before cleaning.
  • Get approval before cutting, drilling or discarding original parts.
  • Keep a parts and modification log for the next owner or mechanic.
