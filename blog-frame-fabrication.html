Inside Our Motorcycle Frame Fabrication Process

Frame changes begin with measured datums, a load-path plan and fixture control, followed by inspection before the motorcycle is assembled.

Apex & Iron Workshop July 12, 2026 8 min read

Frame fabrication is structural work. Even a small change to a mount or rear loop can affect alignment, load transfer and the way the motorcycle behaves under braking and cornering.

In this article

  1. 01Establish chassis datums
  2. 02Design the load path
  3. 03Fixture the frame before cutting
  4. 04Fit, weld and inspect
  5. 05Verify fit with the real components

Establish chassis datums

We record steering-head, swingarm-pivot and axle relationships from the donor before any material is removed. The frame is checked for existing damage and prior repairs.

These datums give the fabrication a reliable reference. Visual symmetry alone cannot confirm that the wheels and suspension will track correctly.

Design the load path

A frame change needs a clear reason and a load path that transfers force into appropriate members. We consider rider weight, engine torque, suspension loads and attachment points.

A rear loop or accessory mount should not place an abrupt stress concentration into thin tube. Reinforcement must be designed, not added by guesswork.

Fixture the frame before cutting

The frame is supported at known points in a jig. Components are dry-fitted where possible so tank, seat, wheel, chain and suspension clearances are visible.

Tack welds hold the planned position while we recheck measurements. Controlled sequencing limits distortion as the joints are completed.

Fit, weld and inspect

Tube ends are prepared for consistent contact and the material is identified before welding. The weld process and filler are selected for the joint and wall thickness.

After welding, we inspect joint quality and remeasure chassis points. Coating begins only after the structure passes its review.

Verify fit with the real components

The engine, swingarm, suspension and wheels are installed or test-fitted to check chain line, alignment, full travel and service access.

A final assembly check catches interference before finish and road testing. The build record keeps the dimensions available for future service.

Workshop checklist

  • Measure the donor frame before disassembly.
  • Check full suspension travel and steering lock during mock-up.
  • Keep structural inspection notes with the build records.
