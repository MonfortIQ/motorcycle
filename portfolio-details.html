IRON EMBER 750 FOUR

A 1978 Honda CB750 stripped of 60 pounds of dead weight, engineered with an 836cc Wiseco big-bore kit, upside-down GSX-R front end, and hand-formed raw aluminum bodywork.

Donor Platform 1978 Honda CB750F
Build Category Bespoke Cafe Racer
Hours Invested 320 Workshop Hours
Power & Dyno 84.2 HP @ 8,500 RPM

CLIENT BRIEF & ORIGINAL CONDITION

The client, an architect from Austin, Texas, brought us a neglected 1978 Honda CB750 Super Sport that had sat motionless in a damp garage for 19 years. The motor was locked, the wiring had been chewed through, and the factory comstar wheels were pitted with corrosion.

The client's directive was clear: "Keep the majestic visual soul of the air-cooled inline-four engine, but give it the stance, suspension response, and braking power of a modern canyon carver. No fake patina, no plastic."

ENGINEERING & FABRICATION BREAKDOWN

Chassis & Frame Geometry

The stamped factory subframe was cut away and replaced with a custom 4130 chromoly looped subframe with a built-in LED running/brake light array. The frame was sandblasted, aligned on our laser jig, and coated in satin gunmetal powder.

Engine Blueprinting & Wiseco 836cc

The inline-four motor received a complete teardown and ultrasonic cleaning. Bored +2mm to accept forged Wiseco 10.25:1 high-compression pistons. Ported cylinder head with oversized stainless valves, web cam performance grind, and Keihin CR29 smooth-bore carbs.

Suspension & Modern Braking

Front end adapted from a Suzuki GSX-R750 using custom billet CNC triple trees from Cognito Moto. Dual Brembo 4-piston calipers biting 320mm floating rotors. Rear suspension converted to custom-valved Öhlins piggyback shocks tuned for the client's 180-lb riding weight.

Exhaust & Handcrafted Bodywork

Handcrafted 4-into-1 stainless steel headers featuring 42 precision pie-cuts, back-purged welds, and an internal reverse-cone megaphone. Fuel tank hand-shaped from 3003 aluminum with knee indentations and a flush aero cap.

Electrical & Motogadget Harness

100% rewiring using the Motogadget m-Unit Blue controller with Bluetooth keyless proximity starting. Micro push-button handlebar switches, mo.blaze disc bar-end turn signals, and an Antigravity 12-cell lithium battery hidden beneath the seat cowl.
